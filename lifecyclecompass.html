Decision-support only — not a conformity assessment. LifecycleCompass assesses completeness and cross-layer coherence of AI-enabled medical device lifecycle processes against a curated, dated standards library. It does not determine conformity, assess clinical/scientific validity, replace risk management or design controls, or constitute a regulatory opinion. Every standard reference carries a status and last-verified date — always verify against the current official document.

Intended Use

LifecycleCompass is a decision-support tool that assists qualified users in assessing the completeness and cross-layer coherence of the development and lifecycle processes applied to AI-enabled medical devices (including SaMD and software that is part of a medical device). It operates on a multi-axis device/AI profile and produces structured observations, gap lists, and traceability views against a curated set of verified standards and process expectations.

Target users

  • Manufacturer regulatory affairs, quality, and software/AI development personnel
  • Independent regulatory consultants
  • Notified Body / regulatory authority assessors (as an aid, not a substitute for independent judgment)

Explicit limitations

  • Does not perform conformity assessment or issue any form of certificate or opinion
  • Does not assess the scientific or clinical validity of data, models, or performance claims
  • Does not replace the manufacturer's own risk management, design controls, or clinical evaluation
  • Does not contain the full normative text of any standard — users must obtain and verify the current official documents
  • Jurisdiction scope: EU MDR + FDA only. UK MHRA, Health Canada, and other national frameworks are not yet covered in this version — this is a stated limitation, not an oversight
  • IVD-specific processes are out of scope
  • Continuous-learning / adaptive systems are supported only at the process-control level; detailed algorithmic monitoring requirements remain manufacturer responsibility
  • LLM / generative-AI-based devices: the tool's primary ML risk standard (ISO/TS 24971-2) explicitly excludes LLM and generative-AI models from its scope. If your device profile involves an LLM or generative-AI component, treat AI-layer risk guidance in this tool as incomplete — no dedicated risk-management standard for LLM-based medical devices currently exists. See the flag in §1 Profiler and §4 Standards Library.

Residual risk statement

Even when used correctly, LifecycleCompass may produce incomplete or misleading outputs if: the device/AI profile is incorrectly entered; referenced standards have been updated since the last library verification; or the user treats tool-generated observations as definitive regulatory findings. The manufacturer (or user organisation) remains fully responsible for the adequacy of its processes and technical documentation. The tool is an aid only.

Who this tool is (and isn't) for

This build aims to be usable by anyone touching an AI-enabled medical device's lifecycle documentation — not only Notified Body assessors. If regulatory jargon in the other tabs is unclear, the Glossary tab (top navigation) defines every acronym used across the tool in plain language.

This tool is not a substitute for legal or regulatory advice, and is not a certification pathway. If you are preparing an actual submission, engage a qualified regulatory professional or Notified Body.

Rule-provenance tag legend

Every criterion across §2, §3, §6b, §6c, §6e is tagged with where it comes from — never left visually indistinguishable:

REG Law / regulation (e.g. EU MDR article, FD&C Act section)
STD Published consensus standard (e.g. ISO 14971, IEC 62304)
GUID Regulatory guidance document (e.g. MDCG, FDA guidance)
DRAFT Draft / emerging standard — not yet final
ED LifecycleCompass editorial judgment — no single normative citation exists for this item; it reflects general good practice, not a rule you can point to in an audit

Score impact of SOUP status

Closes the gap flagged before build: how Module 3 (SOUP) feeds Module 7 (scoring).

When Foundation/third-party model = Yes (SOUP), the AI Data Management Lifecycle and AI Development Lifecycle rings each receive one additional mandatory criterion — SOUP anomaly list & impact analysis on record — and the per-layer weight for those two rings is raised by one weight class (see §7). This reflects that unverified provenance or update behaviour in a foundation model propagates upward through every layer above it.

Foundation-model SOUP items that lack a documented anomaly list or version-pinning policy are surfaced as a High-severity observation in §7, not a routine gap — consistent with the "no risk of overlooking cascading failure" requirement.

Strictly Curated Standards Reference Library

Policy: only entries verifiable against primary or high-quality secondary sources. Every row carries status and last-verified date. Normative text is never reproduced — this library cites metadata and scope only. Verify every entry against the current official document before relying on it.
Excluded until independently verified: any document number not confirmable against a primary source (e.g. an unconfirmed "IEC 63767" reference is deliberately not listed).

Cybersecurity Cross-Link

Per spec §4 Module 8 — lightweight in v1.0.0 by design: LifecycleCompass surfaces where cybersecurity controls attach to each lifecycle layer, it does not duplicate CyberCompass's full 11-jurisdiction clause library. For deep per-country cybersecurity assessment, use CyberCompass (see Compass Family tab).

Clinical Evidence Cross-Link

Per spec §4 Module 8 — lightweight in v1.0.0 by design, mirroring the Cybersecurity Cross-Link: surfaces where clinical evidence obligations attach to each lifecycle layer without duplicating ClinicalCompass's full CER/PMCF/PSUR module set. For deep clinical evaluation work, use ClinicalCompass (see Compass Family tab).

EU AI Act Overlay

Maps AI Act obligation groups onto controls that already exist elsewhere in this tool, and surfaces only the unresolved delta — the design principle is: do not build a second parallel QMS, map into the existing lifecycle wherever possible. Per §1 Profiler, applicability and risk tier can both be set to "Undetermined" rather than forcing a conclusion the profile doesn't support.

Change & Postmarket Control

The nested-lifecycle diagram is primarily development-oriented; AI-enabled devices need explicit control after deployment too. This module covers performance monitoring, drift, change triggers, retraining control, rollback, PMS/CAPA integration, and retirement.

Readiness & Report

A single composite score can let many minor passes numerically offset one fundamental gap. This dashboard instead separates four dimensions and applies non-compensatory critical gates — a gate failure caps the overall readiness regardless of how high the numeric dimensions read. All indicators are editorial decision-support measures, not conformity scores or statistically validated measures.
